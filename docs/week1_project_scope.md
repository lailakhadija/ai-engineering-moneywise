

| Project Name | MoneyWise |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Domain** | Financial Literacy / Consumer Education |
| **Problem Statement** | Young Malaysians often lack the knowledge to manage budgets, credit, and debt. Official guidance is spread across BNM, FEN, AKPK, and other agencies. AI engineering makes it possible to build a document-grounded assistant that explains concepts in plain language with citations and refers users to official services. It does not give personalized advice. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** Financial literacy challenges in Malaysia. Malaysia's youth face higher debt burdens from student loans, store credit, and borrowing from family and friends. Four in five Malaysians seek debt advice from family and close friends. Malaysia aims to increase the financial product information clarity and reliability index from 53.6 to at least 65 by 2030\. **Source/citation:** Financial Education Network. (2025). *Malaysia national strategy for financial literacy 2026-2030*. [https\://www\.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN\_NS2\_ENG\_Interactive\_FA\_LowRes.pdf](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf)  **Evidence 2:** A survey of 1,121 upper secondary students, mostly from B40 communities, found only 25% had high financial knowledge. Only about 37-40% understood investment risk, inflation's effect, or how insurance premiums work. About 90% wanted to learn more, but about 85% found the topic stressful.  **Source/citation:** Financial Industry Collective Outreach. (2023). *Money SENse: Malaysian students' grasp of financial matters*. [https\://www\.finco.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Money-SENse-Report.pdf](https://www.finco.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Money-SENse-Report.pdf)  |
| **Initial Related Work** | **Study / solution 1:** LLM chatbots for personal finance (ChatGPT, Bard)  **Source / citation:** Lakkaraju, K., Vuruma, S. K. R., Pallagani, V., Muppasani, B., & Srivastava, B. (2023). *Can LLMs be good financial advisors?: An initial study in personal decision making for optimized outcomes* \[Preprint\]. arXiv. [https\://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07422)  **Key limitation:** Produced convincing answers but had accuracy and reliability issues, including calculation errors and generic advice. Tested only 13 queries, focused on US financial products, and did not use official documents. **Study / solution 2:** Malaysian financial education resources (FEN portal, AKPK debt counselling).  **Source / citation:** Financial Education Network. (2025). *Malaysia national strategy for financial literacy 2026-2030*. [https\://www\.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN\_NS2\_ENG\_Interactive\_FA\_LowRes.pdf](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf)  **Key limitation:** Despite offering over 800 free resources, more than 80% of stakeholder-engagement respondents were unaware of FEN and the previous national strategy. Resources are scattered across agencies and often static. Stakeholders want personalised, timely financial education integrated into platforms such as online banking. **Study / solution 3:** LLM hallucinations in finance **Source / citation**: Kang, H., & Liu, X.-Y. (2023). *Deficiency of large language models in finance: An empirical examination of hallucination*. arXiv. [https\://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15548)  **Key limitation:** LLMs produced inaccurate or outdated information. GPT-4 scored 82.5% on financial acronyms. RAG improved factual accuracy, but the tasks were narrow and the results may not generalise. Used Wikipedia rather than official financial sources and did not address Malaysian financial information or young adults' everyday money questions. |
| **Engineering Gap** | No single tool answers natural-language questions across BNM, FEN, and AKPK material, with citations and a safe refusal for advice-seeking questions. |
| **Initial Objectives** | O1: To design a document-grounded financial literacy assistant based on official Malaysian sources.  O2: To develop it with retrieval, citations, deterministic calculators, and a guardrail that redirects advice-seeking questions to AKPK or licensed advisers.  O3: To evaluate it on groundedness, calculation accuracy, refusal precision and recall, and latency. |
| **Target Users** | Malaysians aged 18-30, such as university students and first-jobbers. |
| **Data Sources** | Source 1: BNM consumer education pages ([bnm.gov.my/consumer-info](http://bnm.gov.my/consumer-info)). [URL: https\://www\.bnm.gov.my/financial-education-awareness](https://www.bnm.gov.my/financial-education-awareness) Source 2: FEN resources (fenetwork.my).  URL: [https\://www\.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FLCC-for-Malaysian-adults\_compressed.pdf](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FLCC-for-Malaysian-adults_compressed.pdf) |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Document-grounded Q\&A over official public sources, with citations. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | It adds calculator tools, an advice-refusal classifier, sources from multiple agencies, and possibly bilingual (English/BM) content. |
| **Responsible AI Risks** | Risk 1 (Hallucination): Invented rates, fees, or rules lead to a bad financial decision.  Risk 2 (Implied advice): Users treat an explanation as a recommendation, which could cross into regulated activity.  Risk 3 (Privacy): Users paste income, IC numbers, or account details.  Risk 4 (Outdated info): Rules and rates change, so show each source's date. |
